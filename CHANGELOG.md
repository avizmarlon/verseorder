# Changelog

## Unreleased

- Progress: the HUD shows `n/112 watched` next to the ring (`n/112` on phones) and opens a small progress menu with the next unwatched title, Export progress and Import progress. Export downloads `verseorder-progress.json` (`{ app: "verseorder", version: 1, exportedAt, watched: [ids in watch order] }`); import accepts that file or the bare ID array, adds the IDs to the saved progress, skips unknown IDs and reports the counts; anything else is rejected with nothing changed. Watched circles get a tooltip, hover, press and focus states. A one-time hint (dismissal kept in `localStorage`) explains marking a title as watched. Progress wording is plain: `n of N watched` per chapter, “Up next”, “You’ve watched everything!”, “Pick up where you left off” (shown only once something is watched), replacing “lit”, “Every chapter lit”, “Continue at”, “Resume your journey” and “Find your next story”.
- Reword the After Endgame, Spider-Man Multiverse and Marvel Before the MCU chapter descriptions: no quality judgments, and each says why the block sits where it does.
- Mobile (≤760px) HUD: the chapter pips become a horizontally scrolling chip row with all nine names; the active chip is scrolled into view and marked `aria-current`. The bar collapses to the brand dot + chip row while scrolling down (more than 8px per frame, not while the search field has focus) and expands on scroll up or within 60px of the top: 182px → 64px on a 390x844 phone. The HUD is an overlay, so collapsing it shifts no content; transitions are off with `prefers-reduced-motion`. Reaching Marvel Before the MCU from the top takes one tap (section lands 110px from the top in ~1.5s) instead of ~21,000px of scrolling. `--hud-h` keeps the expanded height per viewport width so the result view padding does not jump.
- Split the timeline into nine chapters named after what they contain, instead of four (Genesis, Assembly, Fracture, Echoes): Before the Avengers (1–10), The Avengers Era (11–24), The Road to Endgame (25–44), After Endgame (45–52), The Spider-Man Multiverse (53–63), Beyond the Avengers (64–80), Marvel Before the MCU (81–87), X-Men, Wolverine & Deadpool (88–100) and Back to the MCU (101–112). The order and the per-title `era` labels are unchanged; each boundary sits on an existing era boundary except 10/11, which splits The Avengers Initiative at The Avengers. Each chapter has a new description naming its titles. Chapter anchors are now slugs (`#after-endgame`); the old `#ch1`–`#ch4` links are rewritten to the chapter that now starts at the same title. Progress is keyed by title ID and is unaffected. The HUD shows chapter names only at 1521px and wider (dots with a tooltip below, hidden at 1100px and below).
- Add per-title placement notes in `src/data/placement-notes.json` (keyed by entry id; 100 of 112 titles, obvious chronological spots have none) and render them in the detail drawer under a "Why this spot?" heading via `placementOf()` in `src/components/placement.ts`; no note means no heading. The drawer drops the "Main journey" chip (only "Optional" is shown), shows the slot as `#04 of 112`, renames the spoiler toggle to "More notes · may contain spoilers" and the missing-trailer text to "Couldn’t find a trailer for this one." Add a site-wide "Why this order?" section (`.b-why`) between the hero and the progress line, hidden in the filtered result view. `scripts/data/placement-notes.test.mjs` checks ids, length, uniqueness and banned wording; the e2e covers a title with and without a note.
- Search and filters switch the timeline to a result view: the hero, chapters without matches and their HUD chapter pips are not rendered, the remaining chapters get a one-line label (no sigil, description or progress bar), and the content is padded by the measured HUD height (`--hud-h`). The first match now starts right under the controls (No Way Home on a 390x844 phone: 2,642px → 261px from the top; desktop 1440x900: 3,040px → 181px). The scroller jumps to the top only when the result set changes, never moves focus, and returns to the previous scroll position when the search and filters are cleared. No matches shows `Nothing matches “…”.` instead of an empty block per chapter.
- Remove the "narrative viewing order, not an official chronology" paragraph from the title details drawer (the disclaimer is already in the site copy) and its `.b-editorial` style.
- Add a site-wide social preview: `scripts/og/build-og-image.mjs` (npm `prebuild`) renders `public/og/verseorder.png` (1200x630 PNG, ~215 KB, gitignored) with satori + sharp from the site's own fonts (`@fontsource/fraunces` static WOFF for the wordmark, Space Grotesk). Original art only: VerseOrder text wordmark over the hero disc, key content inside the centre 630x630 square. Metadata gains `og:image` (+ type, width, height, alt) and `twitter:card=summary_large_image` with `twitter:image`, absolute URLs from `site.config.json`. `/og/*` is cached for a day. `tests/social.spec.ts` checks the tags and the image.
- Give every one of the 112 timeline cards a TMDB backdrop banner (per-title focus point) and a self-hosted, muted, looping trailer preview (AV1 WebM + H.264 MP4, 768x320, no audio) served from `https://media.verseorder.watch/clips/`. The detail drawer keeps the click-to-load YouTube embed.
- Preload card media ahead of the viewport. The scroller is the IntersectionObserver root, so the margin applies. Only the start of the next few cards is fetched, nothing loads before the page settles, far cards release their sources, Save-Data is respected, and only one video decodes at a time. On touch, the timeline dot drives playback, held back during flings.
- Serve the clips from the R2 bucket `verseorder-media` through the custom domain `media.verseorder.watch` instead of a Pages Function. R2 answers byte ranges natively (206, `Accept-Ranges`, `Content-Range`), the Cloudflare edge caches the objects (one-year immutable `Cache-Control`), and the bucket CORS policy allows GET/HEAD with `Range` from verseorder.watch. The site is now fully static: `functions/clips` (which used Worker CPU per range request and went over the free-plan CPU limit) is removed, the clips are left out of the Pages deploy, and CSP `media-src` allows the media host. Add `scripts/media/upload_clips.mjs` (MD5-diffed uploads) and the `mediaBase` setting in `site.config.json` (`NEXT_PUBLIC_MEDIA_BASE` overrides it).
- `scripts/media/upload_clips.mjs` no longer overwrites an R2 object whose ETag differs from the local MD5 (the one-year immutable cache would keep serving the old clip). It uploads nothing and exits 1, also with `--dry-run`, and asks for a new versioned file name. `--overwrite` forces it and purges those URLs from the Cloudflare cache. The decision logic is in `scripts/media/upload_plan.mjs`, covered by `npm run test:scripts` (`node --test`, run in CI).
- WebM clips are muxed with cues at the front so the first range request is enough to start playback.
- Add `scripts/media/build_media.py` + `scripts/media/manifest.json`: a TMDB lookup (official videos, textless backdrops, posters), fetch, rating-card/black trim, letterbox detection, encode and data apply. Per-title overrides cover the end-card trim (licensed re-uploads) and crops that cut burned-in streaming watermarks (Netflix, Disney+).
- Fallbacks: titles with no studio trailer upload use an official clip (Luke Cage S2) or a licensed Movieclips / Rotten Tomatoes Classic Trailers upload (pre-2010 Fox/New Line titles), trimmed before the end card. Commentary-only sources (A Funny Thing Happened on the Way to Thor's Hammer) are not offered as the drawer trailer. Iron Man and Iron Man 2 come from marvel.com (Brightcove) and have no YouTube drawer trailer.
- Remove the `/lab` prototype page, its styles and its test.
- Move the website to https://verseorder.watch on Cloudflare Pages. The old GitHub Pages address redirects there.
- Show real cover art for the titles in the timeline, title details and adjacent-title navigation, with the original medallion as a fallback when a cover is missing.
- Add `scripts/resolve-posters.mjs` to resolve cover links (Wikipedia and TVMaze) into the data file.
- Use the standard MIT license text and clarify that titles, characters and artwork belong to their respective owners.
- Add canonical, OpenGraph, robots, sitemap, JSON-LD metadata and a pull-request CI workflow.
